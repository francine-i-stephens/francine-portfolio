About Me

I’m an interdisciplinary data scientist who works across behavioral analytics, experimentation, and spatial analysis. I focus on how people navigate digital and physical environments — and how design, incentives, and context shape their choices. My goal is to turn complex behavioral and geospatial patterns into insights that help teams make smarter, more grounded decisions.

What I Focus On

  • Causal inference, experimentation, and behavioral modeling
  • Geospatial analysis, mapping, and spatial clustering
  • UX analytics and large‑scale analysis of clickstream and agentic telemetry logs
  • Scalable metrics, reproducible pipelines, and ML‑assisted workflows
  • AI-assisted analysis
  • Data storytelling for product, policy, and communications teams

My Background

My experience spans product experimentation at Workday, geospatial and market analysis at Carmel Partners, quantitative UX research at Mozilla, and spatial modeling for the Texas Democratic Party. I’ve built predictive models, standardized experimentation frameworks, analyzed 150M+ behavioral records, and produced geospatial dashboards used for outreach, acquisition strategy, and operational planning. Across all of these roles, I’ve worked at the intersection of behavior, place, and design — helping teams understand how people move through the environments they inhabit and what those patterns mean for products, strategy, and communities.

How I Work

I blend rigorous quantitative methods with clear, human‑centered storytelling. I care about evidence that is not only correct, but useful — insights that help teams understand what’s happening, why it matters, and how to act on it. Whether the environment is a digital interface or a physical neighborhood, I’m motivated by the same question: how can we design systems that work better for customers, users, or stakeholders?
