About Me

I’m a data scientist and product analytics practitioner who works at the intersection of behavioral analytics, experimentation, measurement, and research. I’m interested in how people experience products, what their behavior can tell us, and how better evidence can help teams make smarter decisions. My work combines rigorous quantitative methods with a human-centered perspective. I’ve used experimentation, causal inference, behavioral data, surveys, machine learning, and unstructured feedback to understand what is happening, why it is happening, and what teams should do next.

What I Focus On

  • Product analytics, behavioral analytics, and user journeys
  • Experimentation, causal inference, and measurement
  • UX analytics and quantitative user research
  • AI and agent evaluation using behavioral telemetry and survey data
  • Customer segmentation, predictive modeling, and forecasting
  • Metrics development and scalable, reproducible analytics

My Background

My experience spans product experimentation and AI measurement at Workday, quantitative UX research and product analytics at Mozilla, and geospatial and market analysis at Carmel Partners. At Workday, I partner with product, design, and UX teams on experimentation, behavioral measurement, AI/agent evaluation, customer segmentation, and predictive modeling. I helped scale the experimentation program six-fold, introduced new behavioral KPIs from product telemetry, and developed models and analyses that informed product, monetization, and customer strategy. At Mozilla, I combined experimentation, surveys, clickstream analysis, and unstructured user feedback to understand the Firefox experience. Earlier in my career, I used causal and spatial methods to study markets, mobility, neighborhoods, and the built environment. The settings have changed, but the underlying question has stayed remarkably consistent: how can we use evidence to better understand human behavior and make better decisions?

How I Work

I believe the best analysis starts with the decision, not the dataset. I like working on ambiguous problems where the obvious metric isn't necessarily the right one, where useful behavioral signal is hiding in existing data, or where teams need to understand not just what changed, but why. My approach combines statistical rigor with practical product context. I care about designing good experiments, building measures that reflect real user behavior, finding meaningful patterns in complex data, and communicating results in a way that people can actually use. Whether I'm working with product telemetry, survey responses, customer data, or geospatial information, my goal is the same: turn complex evidence into something clear enough to change a decision.
