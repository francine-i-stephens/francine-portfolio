Precision Pays

A pre‑production A/B experiment revealing how scoring precision reshapes candidate evaluation behavior.

The Problem

Candidate evaluation across Workday’s ecosystem was fragmented: letter grades on one surface, percentages on another, trait‑based scores elsewhere. The inconsistency wasn’t just cosmetic — it made it harder for hiring managers, PMOs, and recruiters to compare candidates confidently, and it obscured the evidence needed to make high‑quality decisions.

Hypothesis & Mechanism

If we replaced legacy letter grades with numeric percentage scores, users would navigate candidate lists more accurately and more efficiently — especially when the task depended on subtle skill differences.

Two B‑tier candidates were deliberately chosen to expose the ambiguity trap. Under a letter grade, Tyler (81%) and Anika (76%) looked identical. Under numeric scoring, the difference became visible — and Tyler’s strong Jira experience mattered for the task.

Say vs. Do

Self‑report suggested both variants were equivalent. But behavior told a different story: numeric scoring produced a 23% lift in accuracy and drove deeper verification — more profile views, more resume checks, more in‑page searches for the Jira requirement.

The Trade-Off

Numeric scoring wasn’t frictionless. Findability sentiment dropped 17 points. But qualitative feedback revealed why: precision prompted verification. Users dug deeper because the interface invited them to. The friction wasn’t a flaw — it was a byproduct of more thorough evaluation.

Business Angle

Numeric scoring didn’t just improve UX. It increased engagement with AI‑generated content — profile and AI‑summary views jumped from 27% to 65%. Under a consumption‑based credits model, this is meaningful usage. The low discovery rate of explainability (13–19%) is no longer just a UX gap — it’s underutilized consumption.

Outcome

The team adopted numeric scoring and initiated a follow‑up A/B test on explainability placement and inline evidence density. The results were shared with other recruiting teams to build the case for standardizing scoring across surfaces.
