Extra-Click Advantage

A pre-production A/B experiment revealing how content placement changes task efficiency and user behavior in mobile onboarding.

The Problem

A manager welcome message was being introduced to a frontline-worker mobile experience, but its placement could change how easily users discovered it. The challenge was to determine which location helped users find and engage with the content without adding unnecessary navigation.

Hypothesis & Mechanism

If the manager message appeared in a more prominent location on the homepage, users would find it faster and complete the task with less navigation.

Three placements were tested: 1) Homepage highlight reel, 2) Announcements card, and 3) For You tab. The experiment measured success, time-to-task, clicks, findability, usability, and confidence — allowing behavioral efficiency to be evaluated alongside users’ perceptions of the experience.

Mental Model Alignment

Behavioral data revealed meaningful differences in how users navigated to the target content. Rather than treating clicks as inherently positive or negative, the analysis reframed them as a findability signal: when users needed fewer taps to reach the intended content, the experience was more efficient. Mobile homepage behavior is influenced by users’ natural tendency to explore unfamiliar content. To separate that baseline exploration from the effect of message placement, the study included a culture-values warm-up task before the primary task. This provided a behavioral baseline for distinguishing genuine findability differences from differences in users’ willingness to explore.

The Trade-Off

Fewer taps were not simply a matter of minimizing interaction. The goal was to make the intended content easier to find without requiring users to learn where it lived. This shifted the interpretation of the experiment from “Which design gets the fewest clicks?” to a more useful product question: How efficiently can users accomplish what they came to do?

Outcome

The experiment gave the team behavioral evidence for how homepage placement affected findability and task efficiency. The results helped inform the design direction for the mobile experience and established a stronger framework for evaluating navigation changes beyond simple click counts.
