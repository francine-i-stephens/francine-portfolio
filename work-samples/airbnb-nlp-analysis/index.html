Airbnb NLP Analysis

This project analyzes 19,967 Airbnb listings across 2,156 Los Angeles neighborhoods to uncover how hosts describe their neighborhoods — and how those descriptions reflect racialized patterns in urban perception. Using NLP topic modeling, dictionary correlations, and spatial statistics, the analysis reveals how neighborhood marketing language aligns with racial composition and longstanding stereotypes about urban desirability.

Download Full Analysis (PDF)

Overview

Airbnb hosts act as place‑based entrepreneurs, marketing their neighborhoods to prospective visitors. This study investigates how those descriptions differ across racial neighborhood compositions — and whether hosts implicitly or explicitly engage with stereotypes about race, safety, and urban life.

Data

The dataset includes 19,967 Airbnb listings from Los Angeles (2022), mapped to 2,156 census block groups. Neighborhood racial composition comes from the 2021 ACS 5‑year estimates. A racial typology (Crowder et al., 2012) categorizes neighborhoods into 12 race‑composition types (e.g., Predominantly White, Majority Hispanic, Black‑White Mix).

Methods

  • Topic Modeling (LDA) — 100 topics modeled using MALLET
  • Dictionary Correlations — LIWC Leisure & Family/Home categories
  • Regression Analysis — Topic prevalence regressed on neighborhood race type
  • Spatial Statistics — Moran’s I and Getis‑Ord Gi* to detect spatial clustering

Key Findings

The analysis reveals clear racialized patterns in how neighborhoods are marketed:

  • Predominantly white neighborhoods emphasize nature, aesthetics, and upscale leisure.
  • Minority‑majority neighborhoods emphasize residents, community, and urban amenities.
  • Majority Black neighborhoods frequently reference safety, diversity, and proximity to major venues.
  • Mixed neighborhoods highlight cultural vibrancy, nightlife, and downtown proximity.
